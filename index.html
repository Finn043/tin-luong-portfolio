A NOTE ON HOW I WORK

I build data systems people can actually rely on.

To hiring teams, founders, and data leaders — I care about the space between raw data and real decisions. That space is usually messy: events drift, definitions change, pipelines break quietly, and dashboards can look polished while the logic underneath is still fragile.

That is the kind of work I choose to get close to.

The Problem I Choose to Solve

In high-growth teams, data is rarely pristine. Operational systems are built for speed, not perfect analysis. Tracking changes, edge cases multiply, and business logic evolves faster than documentation can keep up.

I do not see messy data as an excuse. I see it as the place where a strong analyst can create leverage: by building the translation layer between business ambition and technical truth.

My goal is simple: make the data trustworthy enough that leaders can act on it, and clear enough that frontline teams can use it without second-guessing every number.

Three Commitments I Bring

01

Business-First Modeling

I do not start with a dashboard. I start with the decision being made, then work backward into definitions, grain, schema, and refresh logic.

02

Uncompromising Data Quality

I care about the boring parts because they make analytics dependable: QA checks, repeatable models, clear ownership, and issues surfaced before stakeholders find them.

03

Clear Translation & Enablement

I treat non-technical teams as partners, not request queues. That means plain-language KPIs, documented assumptions, and self-serve analytics people can trust.

What I Bring to Your Team

I am disciplined, iterative, and comfortable working through ambiguity. I bring the same progressive-improvement mindset I use in fitness, strategy games, and learning systems into data work: test, measure, refine, repeat.

Technically, I work across Python, SQL, BigQuery, Snowflake, BI tools, and modern modeling concepts. Culturally, I try to be the person who makes the system clearer after every handoff.

If your team needs someone who can turn imperfect data into reliable decisions, I would be glad to talk.
